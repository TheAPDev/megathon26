Green Ghar Score: M1 Approach

Team Spades · Megathon 2026 · An explainable home energy-efficiency score and retrofit advisor for Indian homes and lenders

1Problem statement and our understanding

Problem. The task is to give every home a transparent Green Home Score (0–100) from the data a lender or homeowner actually has. The score must show why the home scored as it did and which retrofits would raise it, with costs, savings and CO₂ impact. A conversational advisor explains the result in plain language, and a portfolio view lets a bank find, rank and finance its least efficient homes.

Our understanding: what makes this hard in India.

  • The energy problem is cooling, not heating. Load is driven by cooling degree-days, AC star rating (ISEER), west-facing glass, roof heat gain and the AC set-point. Most Western scoring logic assumes heating-dominated homes and transfers badly.
  • Data is sparse and messy. Most homes have no energy audit. What exists is a paper or PDF DISCOM bill (often in Hindi), partial appliance details, an optional BEE star label, and gaps in monthly readings. Self-reported data can contradict the meter.
  • Savings depend on the tariff. Slab tariffs (telescopic, free-unit schemes, category switches) and net metering decide what a kWh saved is worth. LPG cooking and diesel backup carry their own emission factors.
  • Retrofits interact. Fixing the envelope first shrinks what a new AC can save, so package savings are not the sum of standalone savings. Some "green" moves backfire: on today's coal-heavy grid (CEA 0.727 kg CO₂/kWh), switching LPG to induction raises CO₂ unless paired with solar.
  • Trust and compliance. A lender cannot act on a black-box number or on an LLM that invents savings figures. Every number shown must trace back to a deterministic computation, and personal data must stay protected (DPDP Act 2023).

2Idea / abstract

Green Ghar is a hybrid physics + machine-learning engine behind a grounded multilingual advisor.

  • A deterministic India-specific energy model (climate zone, ISEER, envelope, appliances, cooking, backup, rooftop PV) estimates annual kWh and CO₂. A LightGBM model learns the residual between that estimate and real bills.
  • The score is a fixed, published, monotonic weighted sum of seven sub-scores: energy efficiency 25%, consumption intensity 20%, envelope 15%, cooling system 15%, windows and shading 10%, renewables 10%, smart use 5%.
  • Bands: 80+ Highly efficient · 65–79 Good · 50–64 Moderate · 30–49 Poor · <30 Very poor. Every score carries a confidence level and data-conflict warnings.
  • Retrofits are simulated sequentially in interaction order, priced at the home's real slab tariff, ranked by a LambdaRank model, and turned into a payback roadmap with subsidies (PM Surya Ghar) and green-loan EMIs.
  • A hedonic model with conformal intervals estimates the property-value uplift.
  • An LLM advisor answers in English, Hindi and Hinglish, by text or voice, on Telegram. It only calls engine tools; a numeric verifier rejects any reply whose numbers do not match the tool outputs, and a template answer is used instead.
  • For lenders, a portfolio API reports financed emissions (PCAF-style attribution), the worst homes, green-loan target lists and housing-society (RWA) common-solar leads.

Differentiators: bill photo → score (OCR); heatwave-readiness check; sequential, tariff-aware packages; a verified-number LLM; all of it built on free tiers.

Key design decisions

  • Physics first, ML second: explainable, monotonic and works with zero bills; ML corrects the bias where bills exist.
  • Fixed weights instead of learned weights, so a lender can audit and reproduce every score.
  • The LLM never computes. It only phrases tool outputs, and the verifier enforces this.

Data flow for one home

  • Bill photo → 12 months of kWh and tariff → Home record → score with sub-scores and warnings.
  • Top-k retrofit package → ₹/yr saved, CO₂ change, payback, score after → advisor reply.
  • Score is stored in Supabase → appears in the lender's portfolio summary and ranking.
